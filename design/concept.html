overtone

A tray app that floats a small now-playing overlay over whatever you're doing, takes its colours from the record, and sings along in synced lyrics. It works with any player your OS can see.

Three sizes of the same overlay

The pill sits low with the current lyric and its controls. The card is the full player, the lyric panel scrolls line by line, and the tab is the overlay tucked into the screen edge. What the palette engine pulled

The cover is downsampled to 64×64 and clustered in OKLab. Then roles are assigned: base fills the card, accent drives progress and the current lyric, and the three inks are solved for contrast against the base by moving only their lightness, so text stays readable on any cover. This page uses the same palette.

A name

The name should be ownable, short in a menu bar, free of any platform's name, and say something about either the floating or the colour mechanism.

Overtone

Recommended

An overtone is the higher note that rings above the one being played. The app sits over your work and takes its tone from the record. The mark draws the harmonic series as three lyric lines.

Risk: an old open-source Clojure audio library and a few unrelated companies use the word. Run a trademark and app-store search before committing.

Inlay

The printed card inside a CD or cassette case, where the lyrics lived, made in the album's colours. Small and rectangular, like the overlay.

Risk: reads as dental or woodwork without context, and it says nothing about floating.

Lull

A soft pause. It names the feeling of the app: calm, weightless, never asking for attention.

Risk: says nothing about music or lyrics, and the word is crowded among sleep apps.

Ruled out: Sleeve (an existing macOS now-playing app), Lilt (an AI translation company), and Halo, Aura and Drift (too crowded to own).

The icon

The mark is the first three standing waves of a vibrating string, which is literally what an overtone is. They also read as three lyric lines, and each is drawn in a colour pulled from a record, so the mark shows the palette engine at work. The same three waves are the tray icon: in colour on Windows, and as a single-colour template on macOS, where the menu bar requires it.
